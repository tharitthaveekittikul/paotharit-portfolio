In early December 2023, I boarded a bus with 25 students from KMUTNB's Computer Engineering department and headed to Ban Yang Pao School. We had ten days to build something the school had never had: a working computer lab.

I had volunteered for the role of team lead mostly because it sounded like a good challenge. I didn't fully understand what that challenge was going to be until we arrived.

## The Setup

Ban Yang Pao School is a rural school that had received a donation of computers but had no infrastructure to support them — no proper lab space, no network, no one to set it up. That was us.

The work was physical and systematic: running cables, mounting equipment, configuring machines, testing each station. Not glamorous engineering, but real engineering — the kind where something is broken at 6pm and you figure it out before 9am the next day.

## Leading 25 People

Coordinating 25+ students across ten days of hands-on work taught me things no classroom could. When you're the lead, you spend most of your time making sure other people can do their work — not doing all the work yourself.

That means communicating clearly. Splitting the team into groups where skills match tasks. Keeping energy up on day seven when the novelty has worn off and there are still three stations left to configure. Knowing when to make a call and when to ask the group.

The team was genuinely good. Everyone showed up, everyone contributed, and the younger students surprised me with how fast they picked things up under pressure.

## What We Left Behind

By December 10th, Ban Yang Pao School had a fully functional computer lab. Every station worked. The network held. Students from the school came in to try it out before we left.

That moment — watching kids sit down at machines that hadn't existed two weeks earlier — is something I'll carry for a long time.
